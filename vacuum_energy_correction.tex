\subsection{Vacuum-energy correction from the quartic interactions}
\label{subsec:vacuum-energy-correction}

We now consider the vacuum-energy correction generated by the four-derivative
interactions in Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-string-ng-sum-relative}.  The calculation
below makes one additional assumption that is not contained in the
Nambu--Goto Lagrangian itself: the sum modes $X_{\mathrm{s}}^i$ are kept
massless, while the relative modes $X_{\mathrm{r}}^i$ are assigned a common
mass $m$ by adding
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\mathcal L_m
  = -\frac{T}{4}m^2 X_{\mathrm r}^i X_{\mathrm r}^i .
  \label{eq:relative-mass-term}
\end{equation}
The normalization in Eq.~\eqref{eq:relative-mass-term} follows from the
unnormalized sum/relative variables in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sum-relative-fields}.
Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-string-ng-sum-relative} itself has an independent
shift symmetry for $X_{\mathrm r}^i$, the origin of the mass term must be
specified by additional physics.  The result below should therefore be read
as the vacuum diagram generated by the Nambu--Goto derivative interactions in
the presence of the quadratic mass term \eqref{eq:relative-mass-term}; a
microscopic mechanism for the mass may generate additional local interactions.

Let $N$ denote the number of massive relative components.  If every transverse
relative mode is massive, then $N=D-2$.  It is convenient to introduce
canonically normalized fields
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{\mathrm s}^i = \sqrt{\frac{T}{2}}\,X_{\mathrm s}^i,
  \qquad
  \phi_{\mathrm r}^i = \sqrt{\frac{T}{2}}\,X_{\mathrm r}^i .
  \label{eq:canonical-sr-fields}
\end{equation}
The quadratic Minkowski Lagrangian is then
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_2
  = -\frac12 \partial_a\phi_{\mathrm s}^i\partial^a\phi_{\mathrm s}^i
    -\frac12 \partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i\partial^a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i
    -\frac12 m^2\phi_{\mathrm r}^i\phi_{\mathrm r}^i .
  \label{eq:quadratic-canonical-massive-relative}
\end{equation}
After Wick rotation, the massive propagator is
\begin{equation}
  \langle \phi_{\mathrm r}^i(p)\phi_{\mathrm r}^j(-p)\rangle_0
  = \frac{\delta^{ij}}{p^2+m^2},
  \label{eq:massive-relative-propagator}
\end{equation}
whereas the sum-field propagator is $\delta^{ij}/p^2$.

For reference, define the Euclidean invariants
\begin{align}
  A_{\mathrm s} &\equiv
    \partial_a\phi_{\mathrm s}^i\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm s}^i,
  &
  A_{\mathrm r} &\equiv
    \partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i,
  \notag\\
  C &\equiv
    \partial_a\phi_{\mathrm s}^i\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i,
  &
  H^Y_{ab} &\equiv
    \partial_a\phi_Y^i\partial_b\phi_Y^i,
  \qquad Y=\mathrm s,\mathrm r,
  \notag\\
  H^{\mathrm{sr}}_{ab} &\equiv
    \partial_a\phi_{\mathrm s}^i\partial_b\phi_{\mathrm r}^i
   +\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i\partial_b\phi_{\mathrm s}^i .
\end{align}
The quartic Euclidean interaction obtained from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-string-ng-sum-relative} is
\begin{align}
  \mathcal L_{4,E}
  ={}& \frac{1}{16T}(A_{\mathrm s}+A_{\mathrm r})^2
      +\frac{1}{4T}C^2
      -\frac{1}{8T}
       (H^{\mathrm s}_{ab}+H^{\mathrm r}_{ab})
       (H^{\mathrm s}_{ab}+H^{\mathrm r}_{ab})
      -\frac{1}{8T}H^{\mathrm{sr}}_{ab}H^{\mathrm{sr}}_{ab} .
  \label{eq:quartic-euclidean-sr}
\end{align}
The signs in Eq.~\eqref{eq:quartic-euclidean-sr} are worth checking: the
Lorentz-scalar derivative contractions become positive Euclidean contractions,
while $\mathcal L_E=-\mathcal L_M$ after Wick rotation.

\paragraph{Diagrammatic expansion.}
Write the Euclidean action as $S_E=S_{0,E}+S_{4,E}$, where
$S_{4,E}=\int d^d x\,\mathcal L_{4,E}$ is of order $1/T$.  Expanding around
the Gaussian theory gives
\begin{align}
  Z
  &= Z_0\left\langle e^{-S_{4,E}}\right\rangle_0
   = Z_0\left[1-\langle S_{4,E}\rangle_0
      +\mathcal O(T^{-2})\right],
  \notag\\
  W\equiv-\log Z
  &= W_0+\langle S_{4,E}\rangle_0
     +\mathcal O(T^{-2}).
  \label{eq:vacuum-functional-expansion}
\end{align}
The free term $W_0$ is the Gaussian determinant.  For $N$ massive relative
fields it produces the one-loop circle in Fig.~\ref{fig:vacuum-diagrams}(a),
\begin{equation}
  \frac{W_0}{V_d}
  = \frac{N}{2}\int_p\log(p^2+m^2).
  \label{eq:gaussian-determinant-diagram-value}
\end{equation}
The factor $N$ is the component trace and $1/2$ is the usual real-scalar
determinant factor.

At first order in $S_{4,E}$, all four fields at the same spacetime point are
contracted pairwise.  Each Wick pairing therefore makes two propagator loops
that meet at the quartic vertex, giving the figure-eight in
Fig.~\ref{fig:vacuum-diagrams}(b).  There is only one interaction vertex but
there are two independent loop momenta, $p$ and $q$, so this is a two-loop
vacuum diagram.  Dividing Eq.~\eqref{eq:vacuum-functional-expansion} by the
Euclidean volume $V_d$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\rho_{\mathrm{fig8}}
  = \frac{\langle S_{4,E}\rangle_0}{V_d}
  = \left\langle \mathcal L_{4,E}\right\rangle_0 .
  \label{eq:vacuum-energy-first-interaction}
\end{equation}
The plus sign follows directly from $W=-\log Z$; there is no additional minus
sign to attach to the Euclidean vertex.  This two-loop term is the first
correction generated by the quartic interactions and is distinct from the
one-loop determinant, which exists already in the free massive theory.

We use dimensional regularization in $d=2-2\epsilon$ dimensions and an
infinite flat worldsheet.  Define
\begin{equation}
  K_0 \equiv \int_p \frac{p^2}{p^2},
  \qquad
  K_m \equiv \int_p \frac{p^2}{p^2+m^2},
  \qquad
  \int_p \equiv
  \left(\frac{e^{\gamma_E}\bar\mu^2}{4\pi}\right)^\epsilon
  \int\frac{d^d p}{(2\pi)^d} .
  \label{eq:K0-Km-definitions}
\end{equation}
In dimensional regularization $K_0=\int_p 1=0$ because it is scaleless.
Consequently every pure-$\phi_{\mathrm s}$ vacuum contraction is proportional
to $K_0^2$ and every mixed $\phi_{\mathrm s}$--$\phi_{\mathrm r}$ contraction
is proportional to $K_0K_m$; all of them vanish.  Thus, under these specific
infinite-volume dimensional-regularization assumptions, only the purely
massive part contributes,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{4,E}^{(r)}
  = \frac{1}{16T}
      \left(\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i
            \partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i\right)^2
    -\frac{1}{8T}
      \left(\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i
            \partial_b\phi_{\mathrm r}^i\right)
      \left(\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^j
            \partial_b\phi_{\mathrm r}^j\right).
  \label{eq:massive-only-quartic-euclidean}
\end{equation}
This simplification is regulator and geometry dependent.  In particular, on a
finite cylinder the massless sums are not scaleless, so the mixed terms must
be retained when computing a finite-size/Casimir energy.

\paragraph{Evaluation of the figure-eight.}
To turn Fig.~\ref{fig:vacuum-diagrams}(b) into an integral, first contract one
pair of differentiated fields.  If
$U_a^i(x)\equiv\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i(x)$, then
\begin{align}
  D_{ab}^{ij}
  &\equiv \langle U_a^i(x)U_b^j(x)\rangle_0
  =\delta^{ij}\int_p\frac{p_a p_b}{p^2+m^2}.
  \label{eq:derivative-propagator-before-rotation}
\end{align}
Rotational invariance replaces $p_a p_b$ under the integral by
$\delta_{ab}p^2/d$, while $O(N)$ invariance supplies $\delta^{ij}$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
  D_{ab}^{ij}=\delta^{ij}\delta_{ab}\frac{K_m}{d} .
  \label{eq:derivative-tadpole-tensor}
\end{equation}
Introduce
\begin{equation}
  A \equiv
  \partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i,
  \qquad
  B \equiv
  (\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^i\partial_b\phi_{\mathrm r}^i)
  (\partial_a\phi_{\mathrm r}^j\partial_b\phi_{\mathrm r}^j).
\end{equation}
For $A^2$, order the four fields as
$(U_a^i,U_a^i,U_b^j,U_b^j)$.  The three Wick pairings are
\begin{align}
  \langle A^2\rangle_0
  &= \underbrace{N^2K_m^2}_{(12)(34)}
     +\underbrace{\frac{N}{d}K_m^2}_{(13)(24)}
     +\underbrace{\frac{N}{d}K_m^2}_{(14)(23)}
  \notag\\
  &= \frac{N(Nd+2)}{d}\,K_m^2 .
  \label{eq:A2-wick-result}
\end{align}
The first term is
$D_{aa}^{ii}D_{bb}^{jj}=N^2K_m^2$.  Each crossed term is
$D_{ab}^{ij}D_{ab}^{ij}=NK_m^2/d$; the two crossed contractions are distinct
Wick pairings and must both be included.
For $B$, instead order the fields as
$(U_a^i,U_b^i,U_a^j,U_b^j)$.  Its three pairings give
\begin{align}
  \langle B\rangle_0
  &= \underbrace{\frac{N^2}{d}K_m^2}_{(12)(34)}
     +\underbrace{NK_m^2}_{(13)(24)}
     +\underbrace{\frac{N}{d}K_m^2}_{(14)(23)}
  \notag\\
  &= \frac{N(N+d+1)}{d}\,K_m^2 .
  \label{eq:B-wick-result}
\end{align}
Here the three terms are, in the same order,
$D_{ab}^{ii}D_{ab}^{jj}=N^2K_m^2/d$,
$D_{aa}^{ij}D_{bb}^{ij}=NK_m^2$, and
$D_{ab}^{ij}D_{ba}^{ij}=NK_m^2/d$.  These three pairings already supply the
complete combinatorics.  No extra diagrammatic symmetry factor is appended:
the numerical coefficients $1/(16T)$ and $-1/(8T)$ are the coefficients of
the operators in the action, and Wick's theorem enumerates every contraction
of their four fields.
Substituting Eqs.~\eqref{eq:A2-wick-result} and \eqref{eq:B-wick-result} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:vacuum-energy-first-interaction} gives the regulated result
\begin{align}
  \Delta\rho_{\mathrm{fig8}}
  &= \frac{1}{16T}\langle A^2\rangle_0
     -\frac{1}{8T}\langle B\rangle_0
  \notag\\
  &= \frac{N}{16Td}
     \left[N(d-2)-2d\right]K_m^2 .
  \label{eq:figure-eight-general-d}
\end{align}
This form should be kept until after renormalization.  Although its strict
$d=2$ coefficient is
\begin{equation}
  \left.\frac{N}{16Td}
  \left[N(d-2)-2d\right]\right|_{d=2}
  = -\frac{N}{8T},
  \label{eq:strict-two-dimensional-coefficient}
\end{equation}
the term proportional to $N(d-2)$ is evanescent and multiplies the divergent
quantity $K_m^2$.  Setting $d=2$ before expanding the regulated integral would
therefore lose part of the single-pole and finite structure.

To evaluate the loop integral, write
\begin{equation}
  K_m
  = \int_p\left(1-\frac{m^2}{p^2+m^2}\right)
  = -m^2 I_m,
  \qquad
  I_m \equiv \int_p\frac{1}{p^2+m^2},
  \label{eq:Km-Im-relation}
\end{equation}
where the first term vanishes as a scaleless integral.  With the
$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ measure used in Eq.~\eqref{eq:K0-Km-definitions},
\begin{align}
  I_m
  &= \frac{1}{4\pi}
     e^{\gamma_E\epsilon}\Gamma(\epsilon)
     \left(\frac{\bar\mu^2}{m^2}\right)^\epsilon
  \notag\\
  &= \frac{1}{4\pi}
     \left[
       \frac{1}{\epsilon}
       +L
       +\epsilon\left(\frac{L^2}{2}+\frac{\pi^2}{12}\right)
       +\mathcal O(\epsilon^2)
     \right],
  \qquad
  L\equiv\log\frac{\bar\mu^2}{m^2} .
  \label{eq:massive-tadpole-dimreg}
\end{align}
Hence the compact regulated expression is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \Delta\rho_{\mathrm{fig8}}
  = \frac{N}{16Td}
    \left[N(d-2)-2d\right]
    m^4 I_m^2
  }
  \qquad (d=2-2\epsilon).
  \label{eq:figure-eight-compact-result}
\end{equation}
For checking pole terms, the coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:figure-eight-general-d}
expands as
\begin{equation}
  \frac{N}{16Td}\left[N(d-2)-2d\right]
  = -\frac{N}{8T}
    -\frac{N^2}{16T}\epsilon
    -\frac{N^2}{16T}\epsilon^2
    +\mathcal O(\epsilon^3).
  \label{eq:evanescent-coefficient-expansion}
\end{equation}
Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:massive-tadpole-dimreg} and
\eqref{eq:evanescent-coefficient-expansion}, the bare figure-eight graph through
finite order is
\begin{align}
  \Delta\rho_{\mathrm{fig8}}^{\mathrm{bare}}
  = -\frac{N m^4}{128\pi^2T}
  \Bigg[
    &\frac{1}{\epsilon^2}
    +\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(2L+\frac{N}{2}\right)
    +2L^2+\frac{\pi^2}{6}
    +NL+\frac{N}{2}
    +\mathcal O(\epsilon)
  \Bigg].
  \label{eq:figure-eight-bare-expanded}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:figure-eight-bare-expanded} is the expansion of the bare
connected vacuum graph, not by itself a scheme-independent physical vacuum
energy.  A fully renormalized result at order $1/T$ must include the allowed
local vacuum-energy counterterm and, depending on the chosen renormalization
conditions for the phenomenological mass deformation, any lower-order
counterterm insertions required to renormalize the parameters entering the
one-loop determinant.  Therefore a finite constant extracted from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:figure-eight-bare-expanded} alone should not be interpreted as a
physical prediction.

The one-loop circle in Fig.~\ref{fig:vacuum-diagrams}(a) can be evaluated
without manipulating the logarithm directly.  Differentiate
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gaussian-determinant-diagram-value} with respect to $m^2$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\rho_{1\text{-loop}}}{\partial m^2}
  =\frac{N}{2}I_m.
  \label{eq:gaussian-one-loop-tadpole-derivative}
\end{equation}
After $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ subtraction,
$I_m^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}=L/(4\pi)$.  Integrating back with respect to
$m^2$ gives, up to an arbitrary local vacuum-energy counterterm,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{1\text{-loop}}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}
  = \frac{Nm^2}{8\pi}
    \left(1+\log\frac{\bar\mu^2}{m^2}\right).
  \label{eq:gaussian-one-loop-vacuum-msbar}
\end{equation}
It is useful to collect the different orders explicitly.  Writing again
$L=\log(\bar\mu^2/m^2)$, the classical energy of the two strings, the bare
one-loop determinant, and the bare figure-eight graph give, through order
$1/T$,
\begin{align}
  \rho_{\mathrm{vac}}
  ={}& 2T
  +\frac{Nm^2}{8\pi}
   \left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+1+L\right)
  \notag\\
  &-\frac{Nm^4}{128\pi^2T}
  \left[
    \frac{1}{\epsilon^2}
    +\frac{2L+N/2}{\epsilon}
    +2L^2+NL+\frac{\pi^2}{6}+\frac{N}{2}
  \right]
  \notag\\
  &+\delta\rho_{\mathrm{vac}}
   +\rho_{\mathrm{param\,ct}}
   +\mathcal O(\epsilon)+\mathcal O(T^{-2}).
  \label{eq:total-vacuum-energy-bare-through-fig8}
\end{align}
Here $\delta\rho_{\mathrm{vac}}$ is the local vacuum-energy, or equivalently
string-tension, counterterm.  The term $\rho_{\mathrm{param\,ct}}$ denotes
counterterm insertions in lower-loop graphs that may be required when the
parameters of the additional mass deformation are renormalized.

Consequently the renormalized result has the general structure
\begin{align}
  \rho_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\mathrm{ren}}
  ={}& 2T_{\mathrm{ren}}
  +\frac{Nm^2}{8\pi}(1+L)
  +\Delta\rho_{\mathrm{fig8}}^{\mathrm{ren}}
  +\delta\rho_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\mathrm{fin}}
  +\rho_{\mathrm{param\,ct}}^{\mathrm{fin}}
  +\mathcal O(T^{-2}).
  \label{eq:total-vacuum-energy-renormalized-structure}
\end{align}
For bookkeeping, if one simply removes the displayed poles from the two loop
graphs and sets all finite counterterms to zero, their graph-only finite sum
would be
\begin{align}
  \rho_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\mathrm{graph,fin}}
  ={}& 2T
  +\frac{Nm^2}{8\pi}(1+L)
  \notag\\
  &-\frac{Nm^4}{128\pi^2T}
  \left(2L^2+NL+\frac{\pi^2}{6}+\frac{N}{2}\right)
  +\mathcal O(T^{-2}).
  \label{eq:total-vacuum-energy-graph-finite}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:total-vacuum-energy-graph-finite} is not a
scheme-independent prediction: finite choices in
$\delta\rho_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\mathrm{fin}}$ and in the parameter-counterterm
insertions change it.  A physical absolute vacuum energy therefore requires
renormalization conditions beyond the Nambu--Goto derivative interactions.

Thus the perturbative ordering is: the determinant
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gaussian-determinant-diagram-value} is a one-loop effect already
present in the quadratic theory, whereas
Eq.~\eqref{eq:figure-eight-compact-result} is the first interaction correction
and is a two-loop graph of order $1/T$.

Several checks are useful:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Mass dimensions.}
  In two worldsheet dimensions, $[T]=2$ and $[m]=1$, so
  $m^4/T$ has dimension two, as required for an energy density.

  \item \textit{Large-tension limit.}
  The interaction correction vanishes as $T\to\infty$, consistent with the
  canonically normalized Nambu--Goto interactions being suppressed by $1/T$.

  \item \textit{Massless limit in dimensional regularization.}
  When $m\to0$, the massive tadpole becomes scaleless and the figure-eight
  correction vanishes.  This statement is specific to infinite-volume
  dimensional regularization and does not imply a vanishing finite-size
  Casimir correction.

  \item \textit{$N=1$ check.}
  For a single massive scalar, $B=A^2$ identically.  In $d=2$,
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:massive-only-quartic-euclidean} becomes
  $\mathcal L_{4,E}^{(r)}=-A^2/(16T)$ and
  $\langle A^2\rangle_0=2K_m^2$, giving
  $\Delta\rho=-K_m^2/(8T)$, in agreement with
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:strict-two-dimensional-coefficient} at $N=1$.

  \item \textit{Original-field normalization check.}
  Setting $X_{\mathrm s}=0$ implies $X_1=X_{\mathrm r}/2$ and
  $X_2=-X_{\mathrm r}/2$.  Substituting this directly into the two copies of
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:single-string-ng-four-derivative} gives
  $-T(P_{X_{\mathrm r}})^2/64
   +T(G^{X_{\mathrm r}}_{ab}G_{X_{\mathrm r}}^{ab})/32$,
  exactly the pure-relative part of
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-string-ng-sum-relative}.  Canonical normalization then
  reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:massive-only-quartic-euclidean} after Wick rotation.

  \item \textit{Early-$d=2$ warning.}
  The leading coefficient of the regulated diagram is $-N/(8T)$, but the
  evanescent $N(d-2)$ term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:figure-eight-general-d} must be
  retained whenever pole or finite terms are being quoted.  This is an
  especially useful check against an apparently plausible but incomplete
  calculation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Vacuum energy from a general local binding potential}
\label{subsec:general-binding-potential}

The quadratic deformation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:relative-mass-term} is the leading
term in a more general local binding potential.  To compare directly with a
description in terms of the average and half-separation fields, define
\begin{equation}
  Y^i\equiv\frac{X_1^i+X_2^i}{2}=\frac{X_{\mathrm s}^i}{2},
  \qquad
  Q^i\equiv\frac{X_1^i-X_2^i}{2}=\frac{X_{\mathrm r}^i}{2}.
  \label{eq:Y-Q-to-sum-relative}
\end{equation}
The canonically normalized relative field will be denoted by
\begin{equation}
  q^i\equiv\sqrt{2T}\,Q^i
  =\sqrt{\frac{T}{2}}\,X_{\mathrm r}^i
  =\phi_{\mathrm r}^i.
  \label{eq:canonical-q-definition}
\end{equation}
Thus its Euclidean Gaussian Lagrangian and propagator are
\begin{align}
  \mathcal L_{0,E}[q]
  &=\frac12\partial_aq^i\partial_aq^i+\frac12m^2q^iq^i,
  &
  \langle q^i(p)q^j(-p)\rangle_0
  &=\frac{\delta^{ij}}{p^2+m^2}.
  \label{eq:canonical-q-free-theory}
\end{align}
In particular, there is no factor $1/(2T)$ in the canonical propagator.  Such
a factor would appear only if the noncanonical field $Q$ were used directly.
As above, $N$ denotes the number of massive transverse relative components.

Transverse $O(N)$ invariance makes an ordinary scalar potential a function of
$Q^2\equiv Q^iQ^i$.  Moreover, for two identical strings the interchange
$X_1\leftrightarrow X_2$ sends $Q\to-Q$, so the binding potential must be even.
We therefore write the Euclidean potential density as
\begin{equation}
  V(Q)=c_0+c_1Q^2+\sum_{n\geq2}c_n(Q^2)^n.
  \label{eq:noncanonical-binding-potential}
\end{equation}
This is an expansion in the invariant $(Q^2)^n=(Q^iQ^i)^n$, not in the
$n$th power of a single scalar component.  Equation~\eqref{eq:canonical-q-definition}
gives
\begin{equation}
  (Q^2)^n=\frac{(q^2)^n}{(2T)^n},
  \qquad
  \lambda_n\equiv\frac{c_n}{(2T)^n},
  \qquad
  U_{\mathrm{int}}(q)=\sum_{n\geq2}\lambda_n(q^2)^n,
  \label{eq:canonical-binding-couplings}
\end{equation}
where $q^2\equiv q^iq^i$.  The constant $U_0=c_0$ shifts the classical
tension directly.  The quadratic term is treated nonperturbatively as part of
the Gaussian action: matching $c_1Q^2$ to $m^2q^2/2$ gives
\begin{equation}
  c_1=Tm^2.
  \label{eq:binding-quadratic-coefficient}
\end{equation}
These equations take $V(Q)$ itself to be an energy density.  If instead a
handwritten convention writes the potential contribution as
$T\widetilde V(Q)$, then $c_n=T\widetilde c_n$ and
$\lambda_n=T\widetilde c_n/(2T)^n$.  The canonical theory in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:canonical-q-free-theory} and
\eqref{eq:canonical-binding-couplings} fixes the convention used below.

We now compute only the straight-string vacuum energy, setting
$\partial_aY^i=0$.  No correction to the $Y$ kinetic term and no mixed
$Y$--$Q$ derivative operator is included.  For one chosen $n\geq2$, let
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{int},E}^{(n)}
  =\int d^d x\,\lambda_n(q^2)^n.
\end{equation}
Expanding the vacuum functional to first order in $\lambda_n$ gives
\begin{align}
  Z
  &=Z_0\left\langle e^{-S_{\mathrm{int},E}^{(n)}}\right\rangle_0
   =Z_0\left[1-\left\langle S_{\mathrm{int},E}^{(n)}\right\rangle_0
     +\mathcal O(\lambda_n^2)\right],
  \notag\\
  W=-\log Z
  &=W_0+\left\langle S_{\mathrm{int},E}^{(n)}\right\rangle_0
    +\mathcal O(\lambda_n^2),
  \notag\\
  \Delta\rho_n^{\mathrm{linear}}
  &=\lambda_n\left\langle(q^2)^n\right\rangle_0.
  \label{eq:potential-vacuum-functional-expansion}
\end{align}
The sign is consequently positive for positive $\lambda_n$.  No additional
vertex sign or diagrammatic symmetry factor is appended after Wick's theorem
has enumerated the contractions.

\paragraph{Wick-combinatoric derivation.}
At one spacetime point,
\begin{equation}
  \langle q^i q^j\rangle_0=\delta^{ij}I_m,
  \qquad
  I_m\equiv\int_p\frac{1}{p^2+m^2}.
\end{equation}
Write
$\langle(q^2)^n\rangle_0=C_n(N)I_m^n$ and label the two legs belonging to
one selected $q^2$.  If those legs contract with each other, their index loop
gives $N$ times a contraction of the remaining $n-1$ factors.  Otherwise,
remove the selected legs and reconnect their two partners.  The inverse
construction chooses one of the $n-1$ pairs and either orientation, giving
$2(n-1)$ further contractions for every contraction at order $n-1$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
  C_n(N)=\bigl[N+2(n-1)\bigr]C_{n-1}(N),
  \qquad C_0(N)=1,
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
  C_n(N)=\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(N+2r).
  \label{eq:ON-gaussian-moment-product}
\end{equation}

For $n=2$, the three Wick pairings can be displayed directly:
\begin{align}
  \left\langle(q^2)^2\right\rangle_0
  &=\left\langle q^iq^iq^jq^j\right\rangle_0
  \notag\\
  &=\underbrace{\delta^{ii}\delta^{jj}I_m^2}_{(12)(34)}
   +\underbrace{\delta^{ij}\delta^{ij}I_m^2}_{(13)(24)}
   +\underbrace{\delta^{ij}\delta^{ij}I_m^2}_{(14)(23)}
  \notag\\
  &=N(N+2)I_m^2.
  \label{eq:q4-three-wick-pairings}
\end{align}
The recursion then gives the explicit checks
\begin{align}
  \left\langle(q^2)^2\right\rangle_0
  &=N(N+2)I_m^2,
  \notag\\
  \left\langle(q^2)^3\right\rangle_0
  &=N(N+2)(N+4)I_m^3,
  \notag\\
  \left\langle(q^2)^4\right\rangle_0
  &=N(N+2)(N+4)(N+6)I_m^4.
  \label{eq:low-n-ON-gaussian-moments}
\end{align}
For $N=1$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ON-gaussian-moment-product} becomes
$(2n-1)!!$, reproducing
$\langle q^4\rangle_0=3I_m^2$,
$\langle q^6\rangle_0=15I_m^3$, and
$\langle q^8\rangle_0=105I_m^4$.

\paragraph{Generating-function derivation.}
The $N$ components are independent Gaussians of variance $I_m$, so
\begin{equation}
  F(t)\equiv\left\langle e^{tq^2}\right\rangle_0
  =\prod_{i=1}^N\left\langle e^{t(q^i)^2}\right\rangle_0
  =(1-2I_mt)^{-N/2}.
\end{equation}
Taking $n$ derivatives at the origin yields
\begin{align}
  \left\langle(q^2)^n\right\rangle_0
  &=\left.\frac{d^nF(t)}{dt^n}\right|_{t=0}
  =2^n\frac{\Gamma(n+N/2)}{\Gamma(N/2)}I_m^n
  \notag\\
  &=\left[\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(N+2r)\right]I_m^n,
  \label{eq:ON-gaussian-moment-gamma}
\end{align}
in agreement with the Wick counting.

The first panel is a two-loop figure-eight-type graph, the second is a

their loop order.  The quadratic term is exceptional: incorporating it in the
Gaussian propagator resums mass insertions and produces the ordinary one-loop
determinant
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{\mathrm{det}}(m)=\frac{N}{2}\int_p\log(p^2+m^2),
\end{equation}
not a final treatment based on one perturbative mass insertion.

Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:potential-vacuum-functional-expansion} and
\eqref{eq:ON-gaussian-moment-gamma} gives the central result
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \Delta\rho_n^{\mathrm{linear}}
  =\lambda_n\left[\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(N+2r)\right]I_m^n
  =\lambda_n\,2^n\frac{\Gamma(n+N/2)}{\Gamma(N/2)}I_m^n
  }
  \qquad(n\geq2).
  \label{eq:binding-potential-central-result}
\end{equation}
In terms of the coefficient of the noncanonical operator, this is equivalently
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\rho_n^{\mathrm{linear}}
  =\frac{c_n}{(2T)^n}
   \left[\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(N+2r)\right]I_m^n.
  \label{eq:binding-potential-noncanonical-result}
\end{equation}

Using the same $d=2-2\epsilon$ and $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ measure as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:K0-Km-definitions}, the tadpole is
\begin{equation}
  I_m=\frac{1}{4\pi}
  \left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+L
  +\epsilon\left(\frac{L^2}{2}+\frac{\pi^2}{12}\right)
  +\mathcal O(\epsilon^2)\right],
  \qquad L=\log\frac{\bar\mu^2}{m^2},
\end{equation}
and the bare graph is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\rho_n^{\mathrm{bare}}
  =\lambda_n C_n(N)[I_m]^n,
  \qquad C_n(N)=\prod_{r=0}^{n-1}(N+2r).
  \label{eq:binding-potential-bare-result}
\end{equation}
It contains poles through $1/\epsilon^n$.  A renormalized result generally
requires renormalization of the $\lambda_n$, lower-order coupling-counterterm
insertions, mixing among the operators $(q^2)^k$, and the local
vacuum-energy/string-tension counterterm.  Consequently, discarding the poles
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:binding-potential-bare-result} does not define a
scheme-independent physical tension.

The power counting also differs from the derivative expansion.  In two
worldsheet dimensions
\begin{equation}
  [q]=0,
  \qquad [Q]=-1,
  \qquad [\lambda_n]=2,
  \qquad [c_n]=2+2n,
\end{equation}
where $[T]=2$ and $Q=q/\sqrt{2T}$.  Since $I_m$ is dimensionless at $d=2$,
$\lambda_nI_m^n$ has the required energy-density dimension two.  Increasing
$n$ does not by itself suppress an operator; suppression depends on the
hierarchy and natural size of its Wilson coefficient.

For the full canonical potential
\begin{equation}
  U(q)=U_0+\frac12m^2q^2+\sum_{n\geq2}\lambda_n(q^2)^n,
\end{equation}
the potential-sector organization of the vacuum energy is
\begin{align}
  \rho_{\mathrm{vac}}
  ={}&2T+U_0+\rho_{\mathrm{det}}(m)
  +\sum_{n\geq2}\Delta\rho_n^{\mathrm{linear}}
  \notag\\
  &+\text{multi-vertex diagrams}+\text{counterterms}.
  \label{eq:full-binding-potential-vacuum-organization}
\end{align}
The Nambu--Goto derivative figure-eight computed earlier is a distinct term to
be added when both interaction sectors are retained.  The displayed sum is
only linear in each $\lambda_n$; the interacting potential also produces
terms such as $\lambda_2^2$, $\lambda_2\lambda_3$, and $\lambda_3^2$.

If $Q\to-Q$ or transverse $O(N)$ invariance is absent, odd operators can occur
in suitable one-component or tensor-valued theories.  A single odd Gaussian
insertion has zero expectation value, although two odd vertices may contribute
to the vacuum energy.  This does not modify the even $O(N)$-invariant potential
used here.

Finally, if the binding sector enters covariantly as
$\sqrt{-h[Y]}\,V(Q)$, then
$\sqrt{-h[Y]}=1+\tfrac12(\partial Y)^2+\cdots$ implies that a purely potential
contribution $\Delta\rho_n$ to the constant term is accompanied by
$\tfrac12\Delta\rho_n(\partial Y)^2$.  This is a useful Nambu--Goto
consistency check, not an inclusion of the mixed derivative $A$-type
calculation excluded above.
